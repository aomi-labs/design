Notes · 07/24 · one card per slide
1Cover · intro

Hi everyone, my name is Cecilia, and I'm building Aomi Labs — a hosted harness built natively on blockchain. In a nutshell, we operate on the state of EVM and SVM just like Claude Code runs on your OS and filesystem, so we support generic, complicated operations like "find the highest-yielding pool across supported EVM chains, then bridge and stake my money."

That said, it's not impressive to see a crypto agent in 2026 — so why does it matter?

2Problem

The hard reality: enterprises still lack an out-of-the-box solution to automate their operations on crypto. The trend is fintech converging onchain — but if you're a fund manager trying to leverage AI in your allocation, agentic finance isn't solved yet. Zoom into this sector with blockchain and here's everything you'd have to build. Say you want simulation infrastructure to make sure your transactions are safe, bundle that with account abstraction, or send it to Jito for Solana — how much money, time and effort is that going to take you as an enterprise?

3Runtime

Aomi is the SaaS that makes it ready to go. Aside from the core Tx pipeline, we host plugins built by developers and enterprises to bring in their own tools and workflows. These plugins — we call them Aomi Apps — are written in Rust, so they're Turing-complete: flexible enough for your trading-bot logic or asset-allocation scheme.

For example, in our new partnership with Sommelier, they built their liquidity-manager app wrapping their internal APIs; we give them an SDK for WebUI, Telegram, Slack — every product surface — and do the execution for them. They get observability on a Vercel-style dashboard. What would have cost them 6 months became one-shot.

4Architecture

This is the architecture — fairly self-explanatory. Like other AI harnesses, we support multi-threading running in the background so you can trade 24/7. We also did a lot of plumbing around authentication: crypto has self-custody addresses and embedded providers, and resolving those under one account — supporting multiple wallets across multiple threads with access control — actually becomes a hazard. We aim to be enterprise-ready, so all that complexity is hidden from our clients.

5Pricing

This is how we make money — intuitively very similar to Vercel. Hosting one app is $10; we take 10% from tools if an app wants to x402-gate its API. The one thing we do differently from Vercel is outcome-based pricing, based on the state of a contract. Say an app defines the outcome as moving money between two pools and takes 100 bips — we take 30 of those 100 bips. It's basically a sales tax between the end user and the enterprise, based on the outcome.

6Go-to-market

We currently have 1,734 users, 23k downloads, and ~30 apps. We project three wedges for our GTM over the next five years. The trivial case is an onchain agent platform, where hosting takes a big share — but crypto retail doesn't have much growth space. Go for wallet / retail-fintech products and the addressable market gets bigger. The ideal case is becoming the ready-to-go AI solution for digital assets — WorkOS for Auth — where the total market goes up to billions and most revenue comes from outcome.

These are all projected numbers — none of them are real yet, and we're trying to get there. So if you're a developer or enterprise who needs our services, come talk to me.
