02 What it does

One pipeline. Every order lands or never leaves.

aomi runs on a full blockchain client that forks every chain in ~200ms. Every transaction is composed, proven against live state, signed by the end user's own wallet, then broadcast — with account abstraction and Solana Jito built in.

03 Where it fits — and where it doesn't

An honest latency budget.

Reasoning costs time. Simulation costs time. Both are free at some horizons and disqualifying at others, so here is the line rather than a claim you'd have to benchmark to disprove.

Sniping / MEV < 50 ms not a fit  Jito runs parallel auctions on 50ms ticks. A reasoning step cannot compete and we won't pretend otherwise.
Market making / HFT sub-second not a fit  Same reason. Keep your colocated stack.
Intent trading & swaps 2–10 s strong fit  Simulation is free at this horizon and prevents the failed fill.
Background order types seconds → minutes strong fit  Limit, stop, DCA, liquidation guards. Latency is irrelevant; correctness is everything.
Portfolio & treasury ops minutes → hours strong fit  Rebalancing, scheduled flows, multi-step position changes.

Also worth saying plainly: copy execution has a structural ceiling no infrastructure removes — the lead's own price impact lands before the follower does. We'll help you ship it and cap the damage with simulation and slippage bounds; we won't claim to make followers match leaders.

04 The architecture

The keys never move.
Which means a breach isn't a fund loss.

Most execution automation in this market runs on stored exchange API keys or a hosted burner wallet. Both concentrate the blast radius on your servers — and the industry has the incident history to prove it.

"Export Private Keys: Shows your Private Key to export for external use."

— a leading "non-custodial" Telegram trading bot, in its own documentation
A platform cannot show you a private key it does not possess. In 2022 a single bot platform leaked ~100,000 exchange API keys; the resulting class action was revived by the Ninth Circuit on 2 March 2026 and is live today.
Scoped delegation, not key storage

Execution runs under a revocable, permission-limited grant from the user's own wallet — Privy and Para compatible. aomi holds zero keys, and the runtime's counter says so.

Policy is enforced, not prompted

Value caps, allow-listed venues and per-wallet rules are checked outside the model. Published research shows prompt-based defenses fail against memory injection; ours isn't one.

Kill switch & instant revoke

Halt execution or revoke a grant immediately. A failed simulation or an out-of-policy order stops before signature, by construction.

Per-tenant isolation without per-tenant containers

One runtime serves all your users. No container per user — the line item that otherwise scales linearly against you as you grow.
