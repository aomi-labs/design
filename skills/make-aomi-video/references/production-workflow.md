# Production Workflow

## Start With the Claim

Reduce the brief to one sentence the viewer should remember. The claim determines the story; the available animation technique does not.

Examples:

- Partnership: "Somm can package its liquidity logic as an Aomi app and deliver execution through its own surfaces."
- Ecosystem launch: "Aomi is now live on Arbitrum, bringing intent-driven execution to its ecosystem."
- Product demo: "Describe an outcome, review the built transaction, and keep control of the key."

Confirm whether the claim is live, planned, conceptual, or purely demonstrative. Reflect that status in on-screen language.

## Choose an Arc

Use one of these loose structures or invent another when the subject calls for it.

### Ecosystem announcement

1. Establish the chain or ecosystem.
2. Introduce Aomi's arrival.
3. Show what becomes possible there.
4. Demonstrate one legible user outcome.
5. Reinforce simulation, review, or user control.
6. End with a clear co-branded claim.

An "Aomi live on Arbitrum" video does not need an API diagram. It may use chain geography, recognizable ecosystem surfaces, transaction flow, product footage, or a single cinematic interaction.

### Partnership

1. Begin with the partner's product or user.
2. Show the partner-specific problem or opportunity.
3. Reveal Aomi's role.
4. Show the combined experience working.
5. Return value and control to the partner's surface.
6. End on the joint announcement.

### Product capability

1. Start from a plain-language intent.
2. Show the system understanding or gathering context.
3. Make tool use and transaction construction legible.
4. Show simulation and review.
5. Show user authorization.
6. End on the resulting state, not internal architecture.

## Storybook Before Film

Build the storybook as the main review artifact. Each frame should include:

- A representative 16:9 visual
- The beat's on-screen copy
- A short motion description
- Approximate duration
- Any unresolved factual or asset dependency

Use motion inside the frame when it helps the user judge the idea. A subtle loop, camera move, state transition, or short interaction is enough; do not fully animate rejected concepts.

Review the storybook for:

- Whether the first five seconds establish the subject
- Whether every beat advances the claim
- Whether the proof is understandable without narration
- Whether technical detail is legible at playback speed
- Whether the ending states what happened

## Build Long, Then Reduce

Create a comprehensive version when the narrative is still being discovered. After approval, derive shorter edits by preserving the claim, proof, and final lockup. Remove explanatory transitions before removing the proof.

Typical outputs:

- Long explanation: 30-60 seconds
- Announcement: 15-30 seconds
- Social cut: 6-15 seconds
- Insert: sized to replace a segment in another film
- Outro: 4-8 seconds

Treat these as pacing guides, not fixed requirements.

## Delivery Checks

1. Capture stills at beat boundaries and during transitions.
2. Watch the animation at normal speed without scrubbing.
3. Check text at the final delivery resolution.
4. Confirm logos, product names, chain names, and claims.
5. Confirm the last frame holds long enough to read.
6. Render the frame sequence.
7. Encode and watch the resulting video from beginning to end.
